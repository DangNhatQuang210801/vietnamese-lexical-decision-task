## RP2 Literature-Review Notes

- Vietnamese written spacing should be treated cautiously in RP2 stimulus construction: Ha (2003) and Đinh et al. (2008) both support the point that orthographic spaces do not map straightforwardly onto lexical-word boundaries in Vietnamese.
- Nguyen et al. (2025) sharpens the theoretical issue by challenging the assumption that Vietnamese words are constructed from syllables. For RP2, this supports careful item validation: an n-syllable string should not be accepted as a lexical word solely because it is orthographically plausible.
- Vietnamese NLP research has long treated segmentation and syllable-word ambiguity as central problems, from early corpus/statistical approaches (Ha, 2003) through system comparisons (Đinh et al., 2008), toolkits such as RDRSegmenter and VnCoreNLP, and later review work on segmentation systems.
- Psycholinguistic work directly relevant to RP2 remains comparatively limited but important: Pham (2014), Pham and Baayen (2015), Pham et al. (2019), Coto-Solano and Trần (2021), and Verdonschot et al. (2022) provide Vietnamese-specific evidence on visual lexical decision, lexical predictors, corpus frequency, tone/diacritics, and phonological processing.
- The project gap should be framed modestly: Vietnamese NLP research has discussed segmentation and syllable-word ambiguity, while psycholinguistic work directly testing syllable-based length and corpus frequency effects in Vietnamese lexical decision remains limited.
