### Lexical Decision Task and Lexical Access

Meyer, D. E., & Schvaneveldt, R. W. (1971). Facilitation in recognizing pairs of words: Evidence of a dependence between retrieval operations. *Journal of Experimental Psychology, 90*(2), 227-234. https://doi.org/10.1037/h0031564

- Status: verified.
- Verification source: CiNii/Crossref record confirms authors, title, journal, volume, issue, pages, date, publisher, and DOI.
- Use in RP2: Classic lexical-decision/semantic-priming source showing how word/nonword decisions can be used to study lexical retrieval and semantic organization.

Forster, K. I., & Chambers, S. M. (1973). Lexical access and naming time. *Journal of Verbal Learning and Verbal Behavior, 12*(6), 627-635. https://doi.org/10.1016/S0022-5371(73)80042-8

- Status: verified.
- Verification source: Arizona Board of Regents/Scopus-linked record confirms authors, title, journal, volume, issue, pages, date, and DOI.
- Use in RP2: Supports lexical access framing and comparison of naming and lexical decision response times.

Whaley, C. P. (1978). Word-nonword classification time. *Journal of Verbal Learning and Verbal Behavior, 17*(2), 143-154. https://doi.org/10.1016/S0022-5371(78)90110-X

- Status: verified.
- Verification source: ERIC confirms author, title, journal, volume, issue, pages, and date; ScienceDirect confirms DOI and abstract metadata.
- Use in RP2: Directly relevant to word/nonword classification tasks and stimulus-variable effects on response time.

Balota, D. A., & Chumbley, J. I. (1984). Are lexical decisions a good measure of lexical access? The role of word frequency in the neglected decision stage. *Journal of Experimental Psychology: Human Perception and Performance, 10*(3), 340-357. https://doi.org/10.1037/0096-1523.10.3.340

- Status: verified.
- Verification source: PubMed confirms authors, title, journal, year, volume, issue, pages, and DOI.
- Use in RP2: Important caution that lexical decision RT reflects lexical access plus decision-stage processes; useful for discussing interpretation limits.

Balota, D. A., Yap, M. J., Cortese, M. J., Hutchison, K. A., Kessler, B., Loftis, B., Neely, J. H., Nelson, D. L., Simpson, G. B., & Treiman, R. (2007). The English Lexicon Project. *Behavior Research Methods, 39*(3), 445-459. https://doi.org/10.3758/BF03193014

- Status: verified.
- Verification source: WashU Medicine Research Profiles confirms authors, abstract, lexical-decision sample description, journal, volume, issue, pages, date, and DOI.
- Use in RP2: Supports large-scale lexical decision methodology and item-level lexical predictors such as frequency and length.

- Verification source: CiNii/Crossref record confirms authors, title, journal, volume, issue, pages, publisher, and DOI.

### Word Frequency Effects

Brysbaert, M., & New, B. (2009). Moving beyond Kučera and Francis: A critical evaluation of current word frequency norms and the introduction of a new and improved word frequency measure for American English. *Behavior Research Methods, 41*, 977-990. https://doi.org/10.3758/BRM.41.4.977

- Status: verified.
- Verification source: Springer page confirms title, authors, journal, publication date, volume, pages, DOI, and abstract.
- Use in RP2: Strong citation for corpus-derived frequency norms, corpus size/register issues, subtitle-based frequency measures, and frequency as a predictor of word processing.

Brysbaert, M., Mandera, P., & Keuleers, E. (2018). The word frequency effect in word processing: An updated review. *Current Directions in Psychological Science, 27*(1), 45-50. https://doi.org/10.1177/0963721417727521

- Status: verified.
- Verification source: SAGE page confirms authors, title, journal, volume, issue, pages, publication date, and DOI.
- Use in RP2: High-level review for the claim that high-frequency words are generally processed faster and more accurately than low-frequency words.

- Verification source: PubMed confirms authors, title, journal, volume, issue, pages, date, DOI, and nonword lexical-decision focus.
- Use in RP2: Supports discussion of nonword/pseudoword responses and the fact that nonword properties influence lexical decision RT and accuracy.
