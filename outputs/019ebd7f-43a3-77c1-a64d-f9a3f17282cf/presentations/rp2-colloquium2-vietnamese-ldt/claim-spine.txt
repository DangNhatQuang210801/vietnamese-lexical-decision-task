Thesis:
Vietnamese lexical decision is a useful, constrained way to study frequency and syllable-length effects in a writing system where spaces mark syllables rather than lexical words.

Audience:
RP2 Colloquium 2, linguistics/psycholinguistics supervisor and peers.

One-line arc:
Vietnamese orthography motivates the problem; the project narrows it to a controlled lexical-decision design; the pipeline shows clean, reproducible data handling; preliminary mixed-effects results support strong frequency and length patterns with a modest interaction.

Slide claims:
1. This RP2 tests how frequency and syllable length shape Vietnamese lexical decision.
2. The talk follows the full research pipeline, not only results.
3. Vietnamese writing moved from character-based systems to Latin-based Quốc ngữ, but the modern system still raises word-boundary questions.
4. Orthographic spaces mark syllables, so spacing is not the same as lexical-word segmentation.
5. The project deliberately narrows tokenization to a psycholinguistic test of frequency and syllable length.
6. The hypotheses predict faster high-frequency responses and slower longer-word responses.
7. The 3 x 4 within-participant design is balanced at the real-word cell level.
8. Pseudowords make the task a lexical decision rather than a word-recognition-only task.
9. The task procedure is simple, randomized, and keyboard-based.
10. Bias controls reduce and monitor common task artifacts without overclaiming.
11. A demo slide is reserved for the PsychoPy experiment video.
12. The methodology pipeline makes the project reproducible from experiment to model.
13. Cleaning/QC checks protect the analysis against file, mapping, RT, and stimulus problems.
14. QC suggests the data are ready for cautious preliminary analysis.
15. Reproducibility is built through preserved raw data, scripts, cleaned outputs, and R Markdown.
16. Log RT and mixed-effects modeling match the structure of repeated lexical-decision data.
17. The model estimates frequency and syllable-length effects while accounting for participants and items.
18. Descriptively, high-frequency words are faster and longer words are slower.
19. The interaction pattern is visible but should be treated as modest.
20. Model comparison supports adding frequency and length; the interaction improves fit only weakly.
21. Diagnostics do not show a singularity problem, but RT assumptions still need care.
22. Accuracy and response QC suggest participants generally performed the task seriously.
23. The main conclusion is promising but preliminary.
24. The limitations define what must be improved before final reporting.
25. The six-month plan moves from design and collection toward final modeling and write-up.
26. References ground the orthography, LDT, methods, and software claims.
27. Closing.
